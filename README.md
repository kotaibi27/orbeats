# orbeats

Project idea: The Earth Information Jukebox
The Earth Information Jukebox is an accessible web app that turns NASA Earth data into an experience people can explore, play, and compose with. It combines NASA imagery and other visualizations with sonified data, so users can listen to patterns in Earth’s changing systems as well as view them. The app is designed for a broad audience, with particular attention to making the experience meaningful for people with visual impairments.
1. Explore
The Explore tab is the main discovery space. It brings together selected NASA resources, visuals, and sonifications to help users explore Earth’s beauty and understand changes happening around the planet, both positive and harmful. Each experience connects the data to its source and explains what the sounds and visuals represent. Users can explore at their own pace, choosing what to see and hear.
2. Play
The Play tab turns listening into an interactive rhythm game. Players respond to musical patterns based on a selected Earth variable or change over time. Computer vision could let players use movement or gestures to interact, while other control options would help make the game accessible to people who cannot use camera-based controls. The goal is to make Earth data engaging through active listening and play.
3. Compose
The Compose tab is an interactive sound laboratory. Earth sits at the center of the experience, with data variables—such as environmental or climate factors—available to adjust. As users change a variable, the app updates the visualization and the corresponding sound, letting them hear how different values shape the musical result. This gives users a hands-on way to explore relationships in the data.
Accessibility and purpose
Accessibility is part of the experience from the start. The app should provide keyboard-friendly navigation, screen-reader support, clear audio descriptions, captions or text explanations for sounds, and alternatives to camera-based interaction. Visuals should complement the data sonification rather than carry essential information on their own.

In short: the Earth Information Jukebox makes NASA Earth data something people can see, hear, and interact with—turning scientific information into an accessible way to discover, play with, and better understand our changing planet.
